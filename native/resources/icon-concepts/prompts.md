# Hexes: prompts for 24 distinct app icons

Use the **master brief plus one numbered concept** per request. Generating
the icons separately usually gives better silhouettes than asking a model
for 24 icons in a single image. Keep names and option numbers outside the art.

Hexes is a native macOS and Android client for remote AI coding agents,
conversations, and development sessions. Hexes CV is its separate work
profile, not a résumé-building product.

## Master brief — copy this

```text
Design ONE original app icon for Hexes, a native macOS and Android client
for remote AI coding agents, conversations, and development sessions.

CONCEPT:
[Paste exactly one concept direction from the list below.]

Personality: an excellent developer tool with a small, strange soul.
Quirky, clever, a little mischievous, warm rather than corporate.
The result should be distinctive enough to recognize without a wordmark.
Aim for an independently designed Mac utility, not generic “AI branding.”

Art direction: a single confident silhouette; deliberately composed flat
vector-like shapes; a restrained palette of 3–5 colors; carefully shaped
negative space; crisp edges and generous optical spacing. Give the concept
one memorable visual joke, not many tiny accessories. Slight asymmetry is
welcome when it gives the character personality. Avoid stock clip-art.
Use the concept's suggested palette as a direction, not a rigid constraint.

Composition: one icon, straight-on, centered in a square 1:1 canvas.
Keep every identity-defining feature within the central 65% of the canvas,
so a circular or rounded-square launcher mask cannot clip it.
Use a simple full-bleed background. Do not draw an outer platform frame,
rounded app tile, device, desktop, window, shadow beneath a tile, or mockup.
We will apply macOS and Android masks during asset export.

Small-size quality: the symbol must still read at 32 × 32 pixels.
Prefer broad shapes, clear counters, strong foreground/background contrast,
and substantial strokes. No hairline circuits, tiny lettering, dense texture,
or decoration that carries the identity only when enlarged.

Output: one clean 2048 × 2048 PNG, or the highest supported square resolution.
If genuine editable vector export is available, also provide an SVG with
real paths, not an SVG containing an embedded raster image.
No title, caption, option number, watermark, logo presentation board,
photograph, material sample, UI, or collection of multiple icons.
No letters unless the selected concept specifically calls for an H or >_.
Do not reuse the old H-in-a-purple-hexagon logo, the P badge, Chrome branding,
another company's app icon, an AI sparkle, or a generic robot head.
```

### Optional negative prompt

```text
text, wordmark, caption, watermark, numbers, multiple icons, contact sheet,
app screenshot, device mockup, perspective app tile, floating glass tile,
photorealism, generic AI sparkle, brain, corporate cloud logo, stock robot,
microscopic details, hairline outlines, illegible symbols, busy scenery,
muddy colors, weak contrast, copied brand mark, Chrome logo, old H/P badge
```

Remove `text` or `illegible symbols` if the selected direction deliberately
uses a simple H or terminal prompt and the service treats negatives literally.

## The 24 concept directions

### 1. Hex Portal

```text
A precise mint-green hexagonal portal on midnight blue. Nest two or three
bold six-sided forms to suggest a door into a remote workspace. Break the
outer ring with one small coral satellite, as if it escaped its orbit.
Architectural, mysterious, and minimal; no character face or literal H.
Make the doorway's negative space the most memorable part.
```

### 2. Wizard Terminal

```text
A slightly crooked lavender wizard hat whose hatband contains a bold,
accurate >_ terminal prompt. The bent tip and wide brim supply the joke.
Deep indigo background, dusty lavender hat, warm butter-yellow prompt.
Use two tiny, simple spell marks at most. It should feel like a useful
command-line tool that happens to know a little magic, not a game logo.
```

### 3. Hex Cat

```text
A watchful, angular black cat with a softly hexagonal head, enormous
mint-green eyes, exaggerated pointed ears, and one curling tail.
Warm coral background. Clever negative space, compact silhouette,
no whisker clutter, no accessories. Its expression is quietly judgmental,
as if it has already spotted the bug. Graphic and grown-up, not anime.
```

### 4. Little Menace

```text
A tiny lopsided six-sided creature with one huge off-center eye,
two mismatched horns, stubby feet, and exactly one inconvenient tooth.
Charcoal body on sharp pale chartreuse, with a restrained salmon accent.
Think affectionate troublemaker rather than scary monster.
The irregular black silhouette and single eye must work at favicon size.
```

### 5. Pocket Universe

```text
A faceted cobalt-blue hexagonal planet inside a single off-axis
vermilion orbital ring, on warm ivory. One small satellite and one spare
star give it an odd little universe of its own. Flat geometric facets,
not glossy 3D rendering. It should suggest that an entire remote coding
workspace fits inside this compact, slightly impossible planet.
```

### 6. Lucky Die

```text
An oversized isometric gaming die, with three visible faces forming
a compact six-sided overall silhouette. Cream top, dusty pink side,
deep rose side, oxblood background. Use large, readable pips in a playful
one/two/three arrangement. Slightly rounded corners, simple flat facets.
Tactile and lucky, not a casino logo or a photorealistic plastic object.
```

### 7. Packet Pigeon

```text
A compact origami pigeon assembled from five or six confident paper
facets. Oversized cream wing, slate-blue body, coral beak and tail,
pale sky-blue background. The pigeon looks calmly overqualified for
delivering network packets. Strong folded-paper silhouette, one small
eye, no feathers or postal lettering. Avoid a generic paper-plane symbol.
```

### 8. Circuit Beetle

```text
A symmetrical beetle whose wing cases quietly resemble a tiny circuit
board. Mint and sage shell, dark forest background, cream bent-pin legs,
and two warm coral antenna tips. Use only two broad circuit traces;
the insect must read before the electronics. Precise, curious, small,
and mechanically charming. No dense motherboard details or glowing neon.
```

### 9. Honey Hacker

```text
A mischievous bee with a hexagonal honeycomb body and a bold charcoal
bandit mask. Cream wings, honey-gold background, two large ivory eyes.
Keep the body stripes broad and the wings simple. The bee is a cheerful
little operator, not a cybersecurity villain. Avoid padlocks, shields,
hoodies, stingers pointed at the viewer, and generic security-logo styling.
```

### 10. Toast Mode

```text
A squat smiling slice of toast wearing a slightly tilted butter square.
Warm caramel crust, creamy bread, golden butter, pale blue background.
Two simple eyes, a small knowing smile, and barely visible rosy cheeks.
The joke is that this serious developer utility is represented by toast.
Bold rounded shapes; no kitchen scene, utensils, realistic crumbs,
or cutesy packaging typography.
```

### 11. Tiny Kraken

```text
A friendly miniature kraken with a softly hexagonal head and a few
large curling arms, each suggesting a different session it is managing.
Coral creature on deep ocean blue, with warm ivory eyes.
Its tentacles form a compact, recognizable silhouette rather than
a tangle of fine lines. Helpful and slightly peculiar, not nautical
pirate branding, a realistic octopus, or another company's mascot.
```

### 12. Paper Ghost

```text
An eight-bit ivory ghost on dusty lavender, with mismatched rectangular
eyes and one tiny pink pixel blush. Use a deliberate chunky pixel grid,
a stepped head, and an irregular three-point hem. It should have the
quiet charm of an old terminal companion. Flat solid colors, no blur,
scanlines, CRT screen, Pac-Man reference, glossy sticker, or horror mood.
```

### 13. Tangled H

```text
A distinctive H woven from three soft, substantial ribbons:
coral left upright, dusty violet right upright, and a wavy golden
crossbar that passes over and under them. Warm parchment background.
The letter should be recognizable but not typeset. Use flat overlap
shapes to explain the weave. It feels like an orderly knot with a little
personality, not a metallic monogram or an infinite-loop stock logo.
```

### 14. Inbox Imp

```text
A cream envelope that has sprouted two small terracotta feet and
asymmetric horns. Its wax seal is a single orange eye looking sideways.
Muted dark olive background, broad folded-paper geometry.
Make the envelope-and-eye combination immediately readable, with the
horns as a secondary discovery. Quietly strange and useful, not a mail
notification badge, a scary demon, or a busy stationery illustration.
```

### 15. Signal Sprout

```text
A sturdy little green sprout in a hexagonal terracotta pot, with two
broad hand-drawn signal arcs above its leaves. Warm pale peach background.
The pot may carry a tiny simple >_ mark, but only if it remains legible.
Organic and calm, as if the remote connection is a living thing.
Avoid environmental charity branding, realistic foliage, Wi-Fi clip-art,
gradients, and an excessive number of leaves.
```

### 16. Matchbox Rocket

```text
A small vintage toy rocket leaning forward at an optimistic angle.
Cream body, muted red nose and fins, one blue porthole, tiny ochre flame,
dark charcoal-green background. Broad geometric construction and
restrained mid-century charm. One or two simple star marks at most.
The rocket feels pocket-sized and slightly improbable, not a startup
growth graphic, NASA imitation, or photorealistic spacecraft.
```

### 17. Disco Shroom

```text
A squat mushroom with an oversized muted-red cap, unexpectedly square
cream spots, and a tiny face on the stem. Dusty rose background with a
small, quiet checkerboard area near its feet. The square spots provide
the main visual joke. Graphic and eccentric, with a confident silhouette.
No psychedelic scenery, neon effects, gradients, video-game imitation,
or tiny disco accessories.
```

### 18. Rubber Duck Debug

```text
A chunky golden rubber duck in side profile, with an oversized orange
beak and a subtle >_ terminal prompt built into its wing.
Deep desaturated teal background. The duck looks patient and
unreasonably competent. Use smooth, broad shapes, a single bright eye,
and no bath scene. It should work as a simple silhouette without
the terminal detail; avoid a generic emoji rendering or glossy plastic.
```

### 19. Ocular Hex

```text
A spare, watchful eye whose iris is a bold six-sided chartreuse shape,
framed by a dark green hexagon on warm stone. The pupil is a thick
vertical slit, with one small ivory reflection. Minimal and slightly
uncanny, closer to an independently drawn symbol than a mascot.
Strong negative space; no eyelashes, photorealistic anatomy, surveillance
camera, occult text, or detailed heraldic ornament.
```

### 20. Sneaky Raccoon

```text
A compact raccoon face with an exaggerated charcoal eye mask and one
winking eye. Slate-gray fur, warm cream cheeks, muted tangerine background.
Broad angular cheek shapes can subtly echo a hexagon. The expression
suggests it has quietly tidied up something it was not supposed to touch.
No costume, hoodie, tools, detailed fur, badge lettering, or cartoon
franchise resemblance. Keep the mask recognizable at tiny sizes.
```

### 21. Night Moth

```text
A symmetrical cream night moth on dark aubergine, with dusty-pink
hexagonal eye spots hidden in its wings and a narrow pink body.
Restrained ink-cut geometry, broad wing shapes, two simple antennae.
Quiet, nocturnal, and a little mysterious, suited to a late-night
coding companion. No photorealistic insect texture, skull imagery,
gothic lettering, decorative botanical frame, or thin wing veins.
```

### 22. Cursor Comet

```text
An oversized ivory pointer that has become a comet, shooting diagonally
across a muted vermilion field. Three broad charcoal motion trails,
one tiny ivory star, and a decisive arrow silhouette.
The strange transformation is the whole idea. Energetic but composed,
with optical spacing that survives launcher masks. No browser window,
click rings, neon speed lines, generic lightning bolt, or 3D glass cursor.
```

### 23. Noodle Dragon

```text
A sleepy lime-green dragon with a long noodle body tied into one loose
knot. Dusty violet background, a few warm apricot back spikes, two cream
horns, one half-closed eye. Use one thick, graceful continuous body curve
and a compact angular head. Gentle absurdity rather than epic fantasy.
No scales, fire scene, wings, detailed claws, game emblem, thin coils,
or culturally copied ceremonial dragon design.
```

### 24. Liquid Rune

```text
An organic H that looks gently poured rather than drawn with a font.
Deep petrol-blue shape on warm ivory, with a small terracotta droplet
suspended in the gap between the uprights. Broad rounded geometry,
subtle asymmetry, one strong counterspace. Refined but slightly alive.
Flat vector-like construction, not chrome, glass, liquid simulation,
a generic blob, a purple hexagon badge, or a conventional corporate H.
```

## Optional contact-sheet request

Only use this after generating the individual icons:

```text
Arrange these 24 supplied icon images into a clean 4-column × 6-row
selection sheet. Preserve every icon exactly as supplied; do not redraw
or combine them. Use equal optical sizes, a warm off-white ground,
generous gaps, and small numbers 1–24 BELOW the images in reading order.
Numbers are labels on the sheet, never part of the icon artwork.
No extra ornament, gradients, product mockups, or marketing copy.
```

## Optional personal/work pair

After choosing a concept, add:

```text
Create a matching pair for Hexes Personal and Hexes CV, the work profile.
Preserve exactly the same core silhouette, scale, and rendering style.
Use two distinguishable palettes, plus a small structural accent that
still differentiates them in grayscale. Do not rely on color alone.
Do not add P/CV lettering or two different mascots. Return each icon
as its own square image, not as a combined presentation board.
```
